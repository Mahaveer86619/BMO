2. Root Cause: DO Pin Unusable, Switched to AO

DO Pin Stuck HIGH FINDING

The DO (digital out) pin is driven by an onboard comparator whose threshold is set by the blue trimmer potentiometer. Despite rotating the pot in both directions, DO remained permanently HIGH — the comparator never tripped.

  • Confirmed sensor was powered and DO was driving the line (connecting DO pulled GP15 from float to a solid HIGH).
  • The onboard comparator circuit is either out-of-range or the pot has a dead band — the threshold could not be brought below the mic's output level.
  • DO eventually triggered at extreme sound levels (ADC peak ~2200+) but was impractical for normal voice/clap detection.

Fix: Use AO + Software Threshold FIXED

Abandoned DO entirely. Connected AO to GP26 (ADC0) and implemented a peak-detection + software threshold in firmware. This is strictly better for the final BMO product:

  • No mechanical potentiometer to drift or fail.
  • Threshold adjustable in firmware at any time.
  • Peak value is logged — useful for future dynamic threshold calibration.

6. Known Limitations / Next Steps

  • Static threshold: Current value of 250 is below measured ambient noise peaks (~390). Works in a quiet room; may false-trigger in noisy environments. A dynamic threshold (auto-calibrated from a 2–3 second silence window on boot) is the correct long-term fix.
  • DO pin not used: The onboard comparator could not be reliably calibrated via the trimmer pot. AO is the active signal path. DO wire can remain disconnected.
  • No integration with OLED yet: Future step — display trigger status or waveform on the 1.3" OLED from Step 1.
